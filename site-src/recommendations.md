# Recommendations

This page lists Italian-made media that learners tend to get through without giving up. Italians made everything here, and none of it is dubbed. Each item has a rough difficulty and a line on why it helps. The bands are loose. Pick what interests you over what sits in the "right" band, as the [immersion page](immersion.md) explains.

RaiPlay and RaiPlay Sound are the free streaming services of Rai, the Italian public broadcaster. RaiPlay needs a free account, and Rai blocks much of its video catalogue outside Italy, so check a title before you plan around it.

## Start here

If you can't decide, begin with these three.

1. [Podcast Italiano Principiante](https://www.podcastitaliano.com). Davide Gemello speaks slowly about everyday topics, and the site has a free transcript for each episode.
2. [Easy Italian](https://www.youtube.com/@EasyItalian) on YouTube. The hosts stop people in the street, and every video has Italian and English subtitles you can switch between.
3. [Don Matteo](https://www.raiplay.it/programmi/donmatteo) on RaiPlay. It is a gentle detective series with clear standard Italian, and all its seasons are free to stream in Italy.

## Series

### Easier

- [Don Matteo](https://www.raiplay.it/programmi/donmatteo). A priest in a small Umbrian town helps the police solve cases. The dialogue is slow and plain, and each episode tells a complete story. Free on RaiPlay.
- [Che Dio ci aiuti](https://www.raiplay.it/programmi/chediociaiuti). Elena Sofia Ricci plays Suor Angela, a former prisoner who became a nun. It is a light family drama full of everyday talk about work, love and housemates. Free on RaiPlay.

### Medium

- [Il commissario Montalbano](https://www.raiplay.it/programmi/ilcommissariomontalbano). Luca Zingaretti plays a police inspector in Sicily, in films based on Andrea Camilleri's novels. Most dialogue is standard Italian with some Sicilian words mixed in. RaiPlay streams the whole series for free.
- Skam Italia. Students at a Rome high school deal with friends, dating and family. It uses the fast, slangy speech of Roman teenagers. It is on Netflix.

### Harder

- [Mare fuori](https://www.raiplay.it/programmi/marefuori). A drama set in a youth prison in Naples. Many characters speak Neapolitan, so Italian subtitles are useful even for native viewers. Free on RaiPlay.
- L'amica geniale. HBO and Rai adapted Elena Ferrante's novels about two girls growing up in postwar Naples. The cast speaks a lot of Neapolitan, and Rai aired it with subtitles for Italian viewers too.

## Films

No feature film is truly easy, because actors speak at full speed. These are good first choices because the plots are easy to follow.

### Medium

- Perfetti sconosciuti (2016, Paolo Genovese). Seven friends at a dinner agree to read out every message their phones receive. Almost the whole film is one conversation around a table, so you hear everyday Italian among friends.
- Quo vado? (2016, Gennaro Nunziante). Checco Zalone plays a man who will do anything to keep his public-sector job. The jokes turn on Italian habits, and the comedy works even when you miss a line.

### Harder

- Benvenuti al Sud (2010, Luca Miniero). A postmaster from near Milan loses a transfer bid and ends up in a village in Campania. Many of the jokes come from the clash between northern and southern speech, which makes it good practice for accents.
- Io non ho paura (2003, Gabriele Salvatores). Based on the Niccolò Ammaniti novel. A nine-year-old boy in rural southern Italy finds a terrible secret. If you read the novel first, you already know the plot and can focus on the dialogue.

## Books

### Easier

- Favole al telefono (Gianni Rodari, 1962). Seventy short fables that a father tells his daughter over the phone each night. Some take a single page, and Rodari plays with words in ways that make you notice them.
- Marcovaldo (Italo Calvino, 1963). Twenty short stories about a poor worker in a northern industrial city. Einaudi first published it in a children's series, and each story stands alone.

### Medium

- Bianca come il latte, rossa come il sangue (Alessandro D'Avenia, 2010). A sixteen-year-old in Rome tells the story in his own casual, first-person voice. It sold over a million copies and has a 2013 film adaptation you can watch after reading.
- Io non ho paura (Niccolò Ammaniti, 2001). A short novel told by a nine-year-old. The sentences are simple, and the suspense makes you want to keep reading.

### Harder

- L'amica geniale (Elena Ferrante, 2011). The first of four novels about two friends in Naples. The prose is long and dense, but it pairs well with the TV series.
- [Ad alta voce](https://www.raiplaysound.it/programmi/adaltavoce). A Rai Radio 3 programme where actors read whole novels aloud, Italian and translated. It is a free audiobook archive on RaiPlay Sound, useful for reading along with a paper copy.

## Fumetti

### Easier

- [Topolino](https://www.topolino.it/). A weekly Disney comic from Panini. Italian writers and artists have made its own stories for decades, so it is not a translation. The pictures show most of what happens, and the language is simple and playful.

### Medium

- Dylan Dog. A monthly horror comic from Sergio Bonelli Editore, created by Tiziano Sclavi in 1986. Each issue is a complete story in standard Italian, and a long back catalogue gives you plenty to read.

### Harder

- La profezia dell'armadillo (Zerocalcare). The first graphic novel by the Roman cartoonist Michele Rech. It has a lot of text per page and heavy Roman slang, but it shows how young Italians talk to each other.

## Podcasts

### Easier

- [Podcast Italiano Principiante](https://www.podcastitaliano.com). Slow speech for beginners, with a free transcript for each episode on the website.
- Italiano Automatico. Alberto Arrighini, from Brescia, talks about everyday phrases and spoken Italian. He speaks clearly and with a lot of energy. It is free on Spotify and Apple Podcasts.

### Medium

- [Podcast Italiano](https://www.podcastitaliano.com). The main show, for intermediate and advanced learners. Davide Gemello speaks at close to native speed about Italian culture and language. The site has transcripts.

### Harder

- [Indagini](https://www.ilpost.it/podcasts/indagini/). Stefano Nazzi of Il Post retells well-known Italian crime cases in calm, careful Italian. Each episode runs about 48 minutes. The episodes are free to hear and a new one comes out on the first of each month.

## YouTube

### Easier

- [Learn Italian with Lucrezia](https://www.youtube.com/@lucreziaoddone). Lucrezia Oddone, a qualified Italian teacher, mixes grammar lessons with vlogs about daily life in Italy. She also covers idioms and tricky words such as "ci" and "ne".

### Medium

- [Easy Italian](https://www.youtube.com/@EasyItalian). Unscripted street interviews in Milan and other cities, with Italian and English subtitles. You hear how ordinary people speak.
- [Italiano Automatico](https://www.youtube.com/@italianoautomatico). The video side of Alberto Arrighini's podcast, focused on spoken Italian.
- [Podcast Italiano](https://www.youtube.com/@PodcastItaliano). Davide Gemello's channel, all in Italian, on grammar, culture and the way Italians speak.

### Harder

- [Geopop](https://www.youtube.com/@geopop). Italy's most followed science channel, founded by the geologist Andrea Moccia. It explains earthquakes, energy and current events for a native audience, at native speed.

When you find something you like, the [resources page](resources.md) covers the tools for looking up words as you watch and read.
