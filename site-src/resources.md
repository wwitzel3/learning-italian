# Resources

This page lists the tools and sources this guide relies on, plus a few extras. Each entry says what the resource does and who gets the most out of it. Everything here is free unless the entry says otherwise. For what to watch, read and listen to for fun, see [Recommendations](recommendations.md). For the Anki and Yomitan install steps, see [Setup](setup.md).

## Italian learning guides

These sites teach Italian with other methods. Read one or two if you want a second opinion on method, but don't let comparing guides replace immersion.

- [Podcast Italiano](https://www.podcastitaliano.com/). Davide Gemello's site teaches Italian through input, with podcast episodes sorted by level from A1 to C2. The episodes, a short pronunciation mini-course and an ebook on reaching an advanced level are free. His structured courses and PDF transcripts cost money.
- [Italiano Automatico](https://italianoautomatico.com/). Alberto Arrighini argues for learning through listening rather than grammar drills. His material suits learners who can follow slow speech but freeze when they try to talk. He sells courses, and the videos are free.
- [Language Transfer: Complete Italian](https://www.languagetransfer.org/). An audio course that walks you through how Italian sentences are built by asking you to work them out aloud. It runs on donations and costs nothing. Use it in your first weeks if you want a guided way into grammar before native content.
- [r/italianlearning](https://www.reddit.com/r/italianlearning/). A large forum where learners post questions and native speakers answer them. Search it before asking, since most beginner questions already have long threads.
- [Refold](https://refold.la/). A general roadmap for learning any language through immersion. It doesn't focus on Italian, but its stages line up with the ones in this guide and it explains the reasoning in more depth.

## Flashcards and dictionaries

### Spaced repetition and lookup tools

- [Anki](https://apps.ankiweb.net/). The flashcard program this guide uses to review words you mine. It is free on Windows, macOS, Linux and Android. The iPhone app costs money once, and the [Anki manual](https://docs.ankiweb.net/) explains every setting. The [Setup](setup.md) page gives the exact configuration.
- [AnkiWeb shared decks](https://ankiweb.net/shared/decks). Search here for ready-made Italian frequency decks to get your first thousand words. Pick one with audio and example sentences.
- [Yomitan](https://yomitan.wiki/). A browser extension that shows a dictionary entry when you hover over a word and sends it to Anki as a card in one click. Use it for everything you read in a browser. The source code lives on [GitHub](https://github.com/yomidevs/yomitan).
- [Wiktionary to Yomitan](https://github.com/yomidevs/wiktionary-to-yomitan). This project turns Wiktionary into Yomitan dictionaries. Its [download page](https://yomidevs.github.io/wiktionary-to-yomitan/download/) has Italian dictionaries with English glosses and IPA pronunciation.
- [Language Reactor](https://www.languagereactor.com/). A browser extension that shows two subtitle tracks on Netflix and YouTube and lets you click words to look them up. The basic version is free, and a paid tier adds more tools for saving words.

### Monolingual dictionaries

Switch to these once English glosses start to feel too loose. Italian definitions take longer to read at first, but they teach you how natives explain words, and that pays off in your reading.

- [Treccani Vocabolario](https://www.treccani.it/vocabolario/). The best-known free Italian dictionary, with long entries, usage notes and examples. Use it for words where Yomitan's short glosses don't make the meaning clear.
- [Wikizionario](https://it.wiktionary.org/). The Italian-language Wiktionary. Its entries are shorter than Treccani's and often list conjugations, synonyms and etymology on one page.
- [Dizionario De Mauro](https://dizionario.internazionale.it/). Tullio De Mauro's dictionary, hosted by the magazine Internazionale. It marks each word's frequency band, which helps you judge whether a word is worth a card.
- [Garzanti Linguistica](https://www.garzantilinguistica.it/). Garzanti's dictionaries of Italian and of synonyms. You need to register on the site before you can search.
- [Dizionario Italiano Olivetti](https://www.dizionario-italiano.it/). A quick dictionary with short definitions and conjugation tables. Handy when Treccani gives you more than you need.
- [Dizionari Repubblica](https://dizionari.repubblica.it/). La Repubblica hosts the Hoepli dictionaries here, including Italian, synonyms and Italian-English. It is the easiest free way to use Hoepli online.

### Bilingual dictionaries

- [WordReference Italian-English](https://www.wordreference.com/iten/). The most useful bilingual dictionary for Italian, with forum threads under each entry where translators argue over hard cases. Start here in your first months.
- [English Wiktionary](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/Category:Italian_language). Italian entries in English, with full conjugation tables and pronunciation. The Yomitan dictionaries above come from this data.
- [Linguee](https://www.linguee.com/english-italian/). Shows a word or phrase inside real translated sentences from the web. Use it for set phrases and idioms that a dictionary lists without context.
- [Tatoeba](https://tatoeba.org/). A free, volunteer-built collection of example sentences with translations. Search a word to see it used in short, simple sentences.

## Pronunciation

Italian spelling tells you most of what you need, but stress and the open or closed sound of e and o often don't show in writing. These tools fill that gap.

- [Forvo](https://forvo.com/languages/it/). Native speakers record single words, and you can hear several voices from different regions. Recording quality varies, so listen to more than one. You can also add the audio to your Anki cards.
- [DiPI Online](https://www.dipionline.it/). The online version of Luciano Canepari's pronunciation dictionary. It gives the IPA for Italian words, names and places, and marks which forms are modern, traditional or regional. It's for learners who want exact answers on stress and vowel quality.
- [YouGlish Italian](https://youglish.com/italian). Type a word and it plays YouTube clips where people say it in context. Use it to hear how a word sounds inside a real sentence rather than in isolation.
- [Help:IPA/Italian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/Italian). Wikipedia's key to the IPA symbols used for Italian, with English comparisons. Read it once so the IPA in dictionaries makes sense.
- [Italian phonology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_phonology). A longer Wikipedia article on Italian sounds, double consonants, stress and regional variation. It suits learners who like to understand the system behind what they hear.

## Grammar

Look grammar up when a sentence won't parse, then go back to your content. The [Immersion](immersion.md) page explains how much grammar study is enough.

- [One World Italiano grammar reference](https://www.oneworlditaliano.com/italiano/grammatica-italiana.htm). A free grammar reference organised by topic, with explanations in simple Italian. It suits beginners and lower intermediates.
- [Wikibooks Italian](https://en.wikibooks.org/wiki/Italian). A free, open textbook in English that covers articles, verb tenses and pronouns. Use it for a plain English explanation of a topic.
- [Treccani La grammatica italiana](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/elenco-opere/La_grammatica_italiana/). Treccani's grammar, written in Italian and sorted A to Z. Browsing is free. It answers the kind of question natives ask too, such as when to write an apostrophe.
- [Accademia della Crusca: consulenza linguistica](https://accademiadellacrusca.it/it/consulenza). Italy's language academy answers readers' questions about usage in short articles. Search it when two forms both look right and you want a ruling.
- [Treccani Lingua italiana](https://www.treccani.it/magazine/lingua_italiana/). Treccani's magazine section on the language, with articles on usage, new words and common mistakes. Good reading once you reach an intermediate level.
- [Italian Language Stack Exchange](https://italian.stackexchange.com/). A question-and-answer site where you can ask detailed grammar questions in English or Italian. Answers get voted on, so the best ones rise.
- [Coniugazione.it](https://www.coniugazione.it/). Type any verb to see every tense and mood. Use it when an irregular form in a sentence doesn't match anything you know. [Italian-verbs.com](https://www.italian-verbs.com/) does the same job with an English interface.

## Listening

### Public broadcasters

- [RaiPlay](https://www.raiplay.it/). The Italian public broadcaster's free streaming site, with series, films, news, documentaries and children's shows. Many programmes ask you to sign in with a free account, and some are blocked outside Italy. Some shows carry Italian subtitles, which helps with mining.
- [RaiPlay Sound](https://www.raiplaysound.it/). Rai's radio and podcast site, including live radio and archives of past shows. Try [Wikiradio](https://www.raiplaysound.it/programmi/wikiradio) on Rai Radio 3, where each episode tells the story of one event or person linked to that day's date.
- [RaiNews](https://www.rainews.it/). Rai's news site, with short video reports and a live stream of [RaiNews24](https://www.raiplay.it/dirette/rainews24). Newsreaders speak clear standard Italian, so it makes good practice for intermediate learners.
- [RSI Play](https://www.rsi.ch/play/tv). The streaming site of Swiss Italian public television. Its programmes let you hear the Italian spoken in Ticino.

### Podcasts

- [Easy Italian podcast](https://www.easyitalian.fm/). Matteo and Raffaele chat about daily life in Italy at a pace intermediate learners can follow. The episodes are free, and paid membership adds transcripts and vocabulary help.
- [Podcast Italiano](https://www.podcastitaliano.com/). Episodes grouped by level, from slow beginner talks to fast interviews with native guests. Free to listen, with paid transcripts.
- [Coffee Break Italian](https://www.coffeebreaklanguages.com/coffeebreakitalian/). A structured course for beginners, with a teacher and a learner working through each lesson in English and Italian. The audio is free, and extra course materials cost money.
- [News in Slow Italian](https://www.newsinslowitalian.com/). Weekly news read slowly, with grammar notes. Most of it needs a paid subscription. It suits learners who want news before they can follow RaiNews.
- [Il Post podcasts](https://www.ilpost.it/podcasts/). Podcasts from the Italian news site Il Post, made for native listeners, on news, culture and language. Some shows need a paid Il Post subscription. Try [Tienimi Bordone](https://www.ilpost.it/podcasts/tienimi-bordone/), Matteo Bordone's daily show on odd things you didn't know you wanted to know.

### YouTube for learners

- [Easy Italian](https://www.youtube.com/@EasyItalian). Street interviews with Italians on everyday topics, subtitled in Italian and English. Good from the late beginner stage onward.
- [Italiano con Luca](https://www.youtube.com/@ItalianoConLuca). Luca, an Italian teacher, runs a video podcast for learners with Italian and English captions. Suits beginners and lower intermediates.
- [Learn Italian with Lucrezia](https://www.youtube.com/@lucreziaoddone). Lucrezia Oddone, a teacher from Rome, makes short lessons on single grammar points plus vlogs. Suits beginners who like grammar explained by a native speaker.
- [Podcast Italiano on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/@PodcastItaliano). Davide's videos on Italian language and culture, with Italian subtitles. Good for intermediates.
- [Italiano Automatico on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/@italianoautomatico). Alberto's videos, many with his grandmother, in natural spoken Italian. Suits intermediates working toward speaking.
- [Italy Made Easy](https://www.youtube.com/@ItalyMadeEasy). Manu Venditti explains grammar and phrases in English. Suits beginners who want English support. He also sells a paid course.

### YouTube for natives

Move to these once learner channels start to feel slow.

- [Geopop](https://www.youtube.com/@Geopop). Short, clearly narrated science and geography explainers with good visuals.
- [Nova Lectio](https://www.youtube.com/@NovaLectio). History and geopolitics explained in longer videos by a single narrator.
- [Breaking Italy](https://www.youtube.com/@BreakingItaly). Alessandro Masala comments on the day's news four days a week in fast, informal Italian. Good practice for following opinions and arguments.
- [Dario Bressanini](https://www.youtube.com/@dariobressanini). A chemist explains the science of cooking while he cooks. Easy to follow because you can see what he's talking about.
- [Casa Surace](https://www.youtube.com/@CasaSurace). Comedy sketches about a southern Italian family. Expect regional accents and a lot of slang.

## General resources

- [Italian Wikipedia](https://it.wikipedia.org/). Read articles on topics you already know in English. Yomitan works on it, so every article is mining material.
- [Liber Liber](https://liberliber.it/). A free library of Italian books whose copyright has expired, plus audiobooks read by volunteers. Useful once you're ready for older novels.
- [Project Gutenberg in Italian](https://www.gutenberg.org/browse/languages/it). More public-domain Italian books as free ebooks you can load into a reader.
- [Il Post](https://www.ilpost.it/). A news site written in plain, calm Italian that explains the background to each story. Most articles are free to read.
- [DeepL](https://www.deepl.com/translator). A machine translator that handles Italian well. Use it to check your reading of a whole sentence, not to replace reading. The free tier limits text length.
- [italki](https://www.italki.com/). A marketplace for paid tutors and conversation partners. Use it when you're ready to speak. The [Immersion](immersion.md) page covers when that is.
- [Clozemaster](https://www.clozemaster.com/languages/learn-italian-online). A game that drills vocabulary through fill-in-the-blank sentences. The basic version is free. It works as a light extra, not a replacement for mining.

The [Miscellaneous](miscellaneous.md) page covers the Italian certifications, if you need an official level for study or work.
